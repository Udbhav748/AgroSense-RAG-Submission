# Comprehensive Diagnostic & Management Guide: Tomato Bacterial Spot

## 1. Pathogen Overview & Etiology
- **Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*) & Pepper (*Capsicum annuum*)
- **Disease**: Bacterial Spot
- **Causal Pathogens**: *Xanthomonas perforans*, *Xanthomonas vesicatoria*, *Xanthomonas euvesicatoria*, and *Xanthomonas gardneri*
- **Pathogen Classification**: Gram-negative Gamma-proteobacteria (rod-shaped with single polar flagellum)
- **Economic Impact**: Severe foliar blighting, extensive blossom drop, and unmarketable scabby fruit lesions, especially in warm, humid production zones.

---

## 2. Key Symptoms & Field Identification

### Foliar Symptoms
- **Angular Water-Soaked Spots**: Small (1 to 3 mm), dark, greasy, water-soaked spots appear on leaflets. Spots are frequently delimited by tiny leaf veinlets, giving them an angular shape.
- **Shot-Hole Effect & Leaf Tearing**: Spot centers become dark brown to black and dry out. The dead centers often tear or drop out, giving foliage a ragged "shot-hole" appearance.
- **Chlorosis & Leaf Scorching**: Heavily infected leaflets turn yellow, scorch, and drop prematurely, leaving upper canopy fruit vulnerable to sunscald.

### Blossom & Stem Lesions
- Flower stalks (pedicels) develop tiny dark lesions, causing blighted blossoms to abort and drop, directly cutting fruit yield.
- Stems develop elongated, superficial dark cankers.

### Fruit Symptoms
- Diagnostic green-fruit lesions begin as tiny black raised blisters (< 1 mm) surrounded by a distinct pale halo. As fruit expands, lesions enlarge to 3 to 6 mm, becoming brown, sunken, scabby, rough, and crater-like with ruptured margins.

---

## 3. Favorable Environmental Conditions & Infection Pathway
- **Temperature Optimum**: High temperatures: 24°C to 32°C (75°F to 90°F).
- **Moisture & Wind**: Requires driven rain, high winds, or overhead irrigation. Free moisture on leaf surfaces for > 2 hours allows bacteria to swim and enter through open natural stomata, hydathodes, and wind-inflicted micro-abrasions (sandblasting).
- **Survival**: Overwinters on infected crop residue, volunteer tomato/pepper plants, solanaceous weeds, and on/inside contaminated commercial seed coats.

## 4. Organic & Biological Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

| Control Strategy | Specification | Dosage / Implementation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bactericidal Bio-Pesticide** | *Bacillus amyloliquefaciens* strain D747 / *Bacillus subtilis* | 2.0 to 4.0 g/L applied every 5 to 7 days before storm fronts. |
| **Fixed Copper Hydroxide** | Copper Hydroxide 53.8% WG (OMRI Listed) | 2.0 g/L protective spray (caution: copper resistance is widespread in *Xanthomonas* populations). |
| **Systemic Acquired Resistance (SAR)** | Acibenzolar-S-methyl (Actigard 50WG) | 0.25 to 0.5 g/10L (activates natural plant immune defense pathways). |
| **Bacteriophages** | AgriPhage (*Xanthomonas* specific lytic phages) | Applied in late afternoon/evening to prevent UV degradation. |
| **Hot Water Seed Treatment** | Thermotherapy | 50°C (122°F) hot water soak for precisely 25 minutes, followed by rapid cold-water quench and drying. |

## 5. Chemical Spray & Copper-Mancozeb Synergy Protocol

## 6. Prevention & Field Management Checklist

- [ ] **Certified Pathogen-Free Seed**: Verify seed lots are certified tested free of *Xanthomonas* or treated with hot water/hydrochloric acid.
- [ ] **Sanitize Greenhouses & Trays**: Disinfect seedling flats, benches, and tools with quaternary ammonium (Physan 20) or 10% sodium hypochlorite bleach solution.
- [ ] **Field Entry Restrictions**: **Strictly prohibit** pruning, tying, or entering fields while tomato plants are wet with dew or rain.
- [ ] **Drip Irrigation Exclusively**: Never overhead sprinkle tomato fields with known bacterial spot history.
- [ ] **Cull and Destroy Debris**: Incorporate crop residues immediately after harvest to speed bacterial mortality in decomposing plant matter.
