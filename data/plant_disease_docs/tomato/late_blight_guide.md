# Comprehensive Diagnostic & Management Guide: Tomato Late Blight

## 1. Pathogen Overview & Etiology
- **Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)
- **Disease**: Late Blight
- **Causal Pathogen**: *Phytophthora infestans* (Mont.) de Bary
- **Pathogen Classification**: Oomycete (Water Mold) / Stramenopila
- **Significance**: One of the most devastating and aggressive plant diseases in global agriculture, capable of destroying entire commercial tomato fields within 7 to 10 days under favorable epidemic conditions.

---

## 2. Key Symptoms & Field Identification

### Foliar Symptoms
- **Water-Soaked Lesions**: Irregular, pale green to water-soaked lesions appear rapidly on young leaves in the upper canopy.
- **Rapid Necrosis**: Lesions quickly enlarge into large, dark brown to purplish-black necrotic blotches that do not stop at leaf veins. Affected leaves collapse, shrivel, and turn brown rapidly ("blighted" appearance).
- **White Downy Sporulation**: Under high humidity (> 90%) or in morning dew, a delicate white downy fungal-like growth (sporangia and sporangiophores) develops visibly on the underside of affected leaves around lesion borders.

### Stem & Petiole Symptoms
- Dark brown to greasy black, water-soaked necrotic lesions appear on petioles and main stems, causing branch collapse and structural lodging.

### Fruit Symptoms
- Large, firm, greasy-appearing olive-brown to bronze patches develop on green or ripening fruit. The surface remains firm but rough/corrugated, eventually turning mushy if secondary soft-rot bacteria invade.

---

## 3. Favorable Environmental Conditions & Epidemic Dynamics
- **Temperature Window**: Cool to moderate temperatures: 15°C to 22°C (59°F to 71°F). Temperatures above 30°C (86°F) suppress pathogen activity and sporulation.
- **Moisture Requirements**: Free moisture / leaf wetness for 6 to 8 hours combined with sustained relative humidity > 90%. Frequent fog, overcast days, prolonged dew, or cool rains create optimal epidemic conditions.
- **Spread Mechanism**: Millions of airborne sporangia are carried for miles on wind currents. In free water, sporangia release motile biflagellate zoospores that swim to host stomata, encyst, and penetrate plant tissue directly within hours.

## 4. Organic & Biological Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

| Control Measure | Specification / Agent | Application Method & Rate |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Copper Protectant** | Copper Hydroxide (Kocide 3000 / Champ WG) | 2.0 to 2.5 g/L applied every 5 to 7 days before infection occurs. |
| **Bordeaux Mixture** | 1% Copper Sulfate + Hydrated Lime (10:10:1000) | Traditional protective foliar barrier spray. |
| **Phosphite Bio-stimulant** | Potassium Phosphite / Mono- and Di-potassium salts | 2.5 to 3.0 ml/L foliar spray to trigger systemic acquired resistance (SAR). |
| **Sanitation & Rogueing** | Rapid Eradication Protocol | Immediately bag infected plants in black plastic trash bags, seal tightly, and dispose of off-site. **Never compost late blight infected tissue.** |
| **Volunteer Management** | Cull Pile & Volunteer Potato Destruction | Eradicate all volunteer potato and tomato plants and destroy culled fruit/tubers within 5 miles. |

## 5. Chemical Fungicide Rotation Protocol (Targeting Oomycetes)

Because *Phytophthora infestans* is an Oomycete (not a true fungus), conventional fungal-specific fungicides (e.g., standard DMIs) are ineffective. Use Oomycete-targeted systemic and translaminar chemistries rotated with broad-spectrum protectants.

| FRAC Group | Active Ingredient | Commercial Name | Dosage Rate / Liter | Application Interval | PHI (Days) | REI (Hours) | Key Feature |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **FRAC 40** | Mandipropamid 23.4% SC | Revus | 0.6 to 0.8 ml/L | 7 days | 1 day | 4 hrs | Translaminar, cell wall biosynthesis inhibitor (CAA) |
| **FRAC 21** | Cyazofamid 34.5% SC | Ranman 400SC | 0.2 to 0.3 ml/L | 7 to 10 days | 0 days | 12 hrs | QiI respiratory inhibitor; excellent spore kill |
| **FRAC 27** | Cymoxanil 60% WG | Curzate 60DF | 0.3 to 0.5 g/L | 5 to 7 days | 3 days | 12 hrs | Local systemic with 24-48 hr curative "kickback" |
| **FRAC 49** | Oxathiapiprolin 10% OD | Orondis Opti | 0.4 to 0.5 ml/L | 10 to 14 days | 0 days | 4 hrs | Extremely potent oxysterol-binding protein inhibitor |
| **FRAC 43** | Fluopicolide 39.5% SC | Presidio | 0.3 to 0.4 ml/L | 7 to 10 days | 2 days | 12 hrs | Disrupts spectrin-like cytoskeleton |
| **FRAC M05** | Chlorothalonil 75% WP | Bravo Weather Stik | 2.0 to 2.5 g/L | 5 to 7 days | 7 days | 12 hrs | Essential multi-site tank mix partner |

### Resistance Management Protocol
- **Tank Mix Rule**: Always tank mix single-site Oomycete chemistries (FRAC 40, 21, 27, 49) with a multi-site protectant (Chlorothalonil FRAC M05 or Mancozeb FRAC M03).
- **Sequential Limit**: Never apply more than two consecutive sprays of the same FRAC group.

## 6. Prevention & Field Management Checklist

- [ ] **Disease-Free Seedlings**: Purchase only certified, inspected transplants; reject shipments showing dark petiole spots or stem water-soaking.
- [ ] **Resistant Cultivars**: Plant triple-resistant tomato varieties with *Ph-2* and *Ph-3* resistance genes (e.g., 'Mountain Magic', 'Defiant PhR', 'Plum Regal', 'Stellar', 'Iron Lady').
- [ ] **Regional Blight Alert Monitoring**: Check regional blight forecasting networks (e.g., USAblight / Blightwatch) weekly; initiate protective spray schedules immediately when alerts are issued.
- [ ] **Canopy Ventilation**: Stake plants cleanly, eliminate weeds, and avoid ultra-dense planting arrangements.
- [ ] **Zero Overhead Sprinkling**: Use drip irrigation only; irrigate early morning if furrow or overhead watering is unavoidable.
