# Comprehensive Diagnostic & Management Guide: Tomato Early Blight

## 1. Pathogen Overview & Etiology
- **Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)
- **Disease**: Early Blight
- **Causal Pathogens**: *Alternaria linariae* (formerly *Alternaria solani* f. sp. *lycopersici*) and *Alternaria solani*
- **Pathogen Classification**: Ascomycete / Hyphomycete Fungus
- **Host Range**: Solanaceous crops including tomato, potato, eggplant, and solanaceous weeds (e.g., black nightshade, horsenettle).

---

## 2. Key Symptoms & Field Identification

### Foliar Symptoms
- **Target-Board Lesions**: Small brown-to-black spots (1 to 2 mm) appear initially on older, lower foliage closest to the soil line. As lesions enlarge (up to 15 mm), they develop distinctive **concentric rings** resembling a bullseye or target board.
- **Chlorotic Halo**: Lesions are frequently surrounded by a prominent yellow (chlorotic) zone caused by fungal alternariol toxins.
- **Defoliation**: Extensive lesion coalescence leads to leaf yellowing, necrosis, premature senescence, and upward defoliation, exposing developing fruit to solar injury (sunscald).

### Stem & Collar Rot Symptoms
- **Seedlings**: Dark, sunken collar rot lesions girdle the hypocotyl at the soil line, causing damping-off or stunting.
- **Mature Stems**: Dark, elongated, sunken lesions with concentric banding develop at branch junctions and stem nodes.

### Fruit Symptoms
- Sunken, leathery, dark brown to black rot develops at the stem-end calyx attachment. Lesions exhibit distinct concentric rings and may be colonized by black velvety fungal spores in humid conditions.

---

## 3. Favorable Environmental Conditions & Disease Cycle
- **Optimal Temperature Range**: 24°C to 29°C (75°F to 84°F). The fungus can grow and sporulate between 10°C and 35°C.
- **Moisture Requirements**: Requires free moisture (leaf wetness from rain, overhead irrigation, or heavy dew) for a minimum of 2 to 4 hours for germination, and sustained relative humidity > 90% for rapid lesion expansion.
- **Overwintering & Spread**: Survives as dormant mycelium and conidia in infected crop residue, solanaceous weed hosts, volunteer tomatoes, and contaminated seed. Spores are disseminated by wind, splashing rain, overhead irrigation, and farm equipment.

---

## 4. Organic & Biological Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

| Control Measure | Specification / Agent | Application Method & Timing |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bio-fungicide** | *Bacillus subtilis* (Serenade ASO / Cease) | Foliar spray at 4 to 8 ml/L every 7 days prior to disease onset. Colonizes leaf surfaces and produces antifungal lipopeptides. |
| **Bio-fungicide** | *Bacillus amyloliquefaciens* strain D747 (Double Nickel) | 2 to 4 g/L preventative foliar drench. |
| **Copper Bactericide/Fungicide** | Copper Octanoate / Copper Hydroxide (OMRI Listed) | 2.0 to 2.5 g/L applied every 7 to 10 days. |
| **Potassium Bicarbonate** | *MilStop* / Armicarb (Potassium Bicarbonate 85%) | 3.0 to 4.0 g/L spray on active foliage to raise surface pH and disrupt fungal cell walls. |
| **Cultural Sanitation** | Lower Foliage Pruning | Prune bottom 12 inches (30 cm) of leaves once plants reach 24 inches to eliminate splash-zone contact with soil. |
| **Mulching** | Black woven landscape fabric or clean wheat straw | Apply 2 to 3-inch layer across planting beds to physically block soil splash containing *Alternaria* conidia. |

## 5. Chemical Fungicide Rotation Protocol

To prevent fungicide resistance, alternate chemical classes according to **FRAC (Fungicide Resistance Action Committee)** codes. Never make more than two consecutive applications of single-site penetrant fungicides (e.g., FRAC 11 or FRAC 7).

| FRAC Group | Active Ingredient | Trade Formulation | Dosage Rate / Liter | Application Interval | PHI (Days) | REI (Hours) | Mode of Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **FRAC M05** | Chlorothalonil 75% WP | Bravo Weather Stik | 2.0 g/L | 7 to 10 days | 7 days | 12 hrs | Multi-site contact protectant |
| **FRAC 11** | Azoxystrobin 23% SC | Quadris Flowable | 0.8 to 1.0 ml/L | 10 to 14 days | 0 days | 4 hrs | Quinone outside inhibitor (QoI); cellular respiration inhibitor |
| **FRAC 3** | Difenoconazole 25% EC | Inspire Super / Score | 0.5 ml/L | 10 to 14 days | 7 days | 12 hrs | Demethylation inhibitor (DMI); sterol biosynthesis |
| **FRAC 7 + 11** | Fluxapyroxad + Pyraclostrobin | Merivon SC | 0.4 to 0.6 ml/L | 14 days | 0 days | 12 hrs | Dual-action succinate dehydrogenase inhibitor + QoI |
| **FRAC M03** | Mancozeb 75% WP | Dithane M-45 / Manzate | 2.0 to 2.5 g/L | 7 to 10 days | 5 days | 24 hrs | Multi-site dithiocarbamate protectant |

### Recommended Rotation Schedule
1. **Week 1 (Early vegetative)**: Protectant base with Chlorothalonil (FRAC M05) or Mancozeb (FRAC M03).
2. **Week 2-3 (First flowering / fruit set)**: Systemic penetrant Difenoconazole (FRAC 3) or Quadris (FRAC 11).
3. **Week 4 (Mid fruit sizing)**: Multi-site protectant (FRAC M05) tank-mixed with *Bacillus subtilis*.
4. **Week 5 (Harvest onset)**: Merivon (FRAC 7 + 11) or Quadris (0-day PHI) rotated back to protectant.

---

## 6. Prevention & Field Management Checklist

- [ ] **Crop Rotation**: Enforce a strict 3 to 4-year rotation away from all Solanaceous crops (tomatoes, potatoes, peppers, eggplants).
- [ ] **Resistant Varieties**: Select tomato cultivars with demonstrated genetic resistance or tolerance (carrying *Ad-1*, *Ad-2*, or *Ph-2*/*Ph-3* combined genes, such as 'Defiant PhR', 'Mountain Merit', 'Mountain Magic', 'Iron Lady').
- [ ] **Drip Irrigation**: Utilize sub-surface or surface drip lines exclusively. Avoid overhead sprinkler irrigation to keep leaf wetness duration near zero.
- [ ] **Air Circulation & Staking**: Stake, trellis, or cage indeterminate tomatoes. Maintain 18 to 24-inch in-row spacing and 4 to 5-foot between-row spacing.
- [ ] **Post-Harvest Cleanup**: Deep-till crop debris immediately post-harvest to accelerate microbial decomposition, or remove and destroy vines from the field.
