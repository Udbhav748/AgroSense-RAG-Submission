# Comprehensive Diagnostic & Management Guide: Tomato Septoria Leaf Spot

## 1. Pathogen Overview & Etiology
- **Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)
- **Disease**: Septoria Leaf Spot
- **Causal Pathogen**: *Septoria lycopersici* Speg.
- **Pathogen Classification**: Ascomycete / Sphaeropsidales
- **Key Distinguishing Feature**: Numerous tiny circular spots with grey centers and distinct dark brown borders, studded with microscopic black fruiting bodies (pycnidia).

---

## 2. Key Symptoms & Field Identification
- **Foliar Lesions**: Abundant small circular spots (2 to 4 mm in diameter) develop on the oldest lower leaves.
- **Lesion Morphology**: Ashen-gray to tan center encircled by a sharp dark brown to black margin, surrounded by a narrow yellow halo.
- **Pycnidia (Diagnostic Diagnostic Sign)**: Using a 10x hand lens, tiny black specks resembling ground black pepper can be clearly seen embedded in the grey lesion centers. These are fungal pycnidia that extrude gelatinous tendrils of conidia during rain.
- **Progressive Defoliation**: Spots coalesce rapidly, causing individual leaves to yellow, wither into brown parchment, and drop. The disease ascends the vine rapidly, leaving bare stems with unripened fruit exposed to sunscald.

---

## 3. Favorable Environmental Conditions & Lifecycle
- **Temperature Window**: 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Leaf Wetness & Rain Splash**: Requires prolonged leaf wetness (6 to 12 hours) and relative humidity > 90%. Rain droplets or splashing irrigation physically dislodge pycnidiospores and transport them upward from soil residue onto lower foliage.
- **Overwintering**: Persists on Solanaceous crop debris, horse nettle (*Solanum carolinense*), and jimsonweed.

---

## 4. Control & Management Protocols

| Category | Measure / Chemical | Dosage / Rate | Timing & Instructions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cultural** | Heavy Mulching (straw/plastic) | 5 to 8 cm depth | Prevents upward spore splashing from infested soil. |
| **Cultural** | Bottom Leaf Pruning | Bottom 30 cm | Remove lower suckers and leaves once fruit clusters form. |
| **Protectant** | Chlorothalonil 75% WP | 2.0 g/L | Spray every 7 to 10 days starting at transplant establishment. |
| **Protectant** | Mancozeb 75% WP | 2.0 to 2.5 g/L | Broad-spectrum multi-site protectant. 5-day PHI. |
| **Systemic** | Difenoconazole 25% EC | 0.5 ml/L | FRAC 3 translaminar systemic curative and protectant. |
| **Organic** | Copper Hydroxide (Kocide) | 2.0 g/L | Apply prior to rainy periods. |
