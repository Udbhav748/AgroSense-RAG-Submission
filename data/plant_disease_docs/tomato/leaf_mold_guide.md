# Comprehensive Diagnostic & Management Guide: Tomato Leaf Mold

## 1. Pathogen Overview & Etiology
- **Crop**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*)
- **Disease**: Leaf Mold
- **Causal Pathogen**: *Passalora fulva* (formerly *Cladosporium fulvum*)
- **Pathogen Classification**: Ascomycete / Mycosphaerellaceae
- **Primary Setting**: High tunnels, plastic hoop houses, and commercial glass greenhouses with high relative humidity and reduced ventilation.

---

## 2. Key Symptoms & Field Identification
- **Upper Leaf Surface**: Pale yellow, diffuse, chlorotic spots with indistinct margins appear on the upper surface of older lower leaves.
- **Lower Leaf Surface (Diagnostic)**: A dense, olive-green to brownish-purple velvety fungal mat develops directly beneath the yellow patches on the lower leaf surface. This velvety layer consists of thousands of conidiophores and conidia.
- **Canopy Progression**: Leaves curl upward, turn completely yellow, wither, and drop. In severe greenhouse infestations, defoliation spreads from lower canopy to the crown, arresting fruit sizing.

---

## 3. Favorable Environmental Conditions & Management Controls
- **Relative Humidity (Critical)**: Thrives in relative humidity > 85%. Pathogen sporulation and germination cease if RH is maintained strictly below 80%.
- **Optimal Temperature**: 21°C to 24°C (70°F to 75°F).
- **IPM Environmental Management**:
  - Run horizontal airflow (HAF) fans continuously to prevent stagnant microclimates within plant canopies.
  - Vent and heat high tunnels in early evening and dawn to purge moisture and keep temperature above dew point.
  - Increase plant spacing (minimum 24 inches in-row) and prune lower suckers/leaves.
