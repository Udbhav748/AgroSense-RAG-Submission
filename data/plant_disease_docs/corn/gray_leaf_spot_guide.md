# Comprehensive Diagnostic & Management Guide: Corn Gray Leaf Spot

## 1. Pathogen Overview & Etiology
- **Crop**: Field Corn & Seed Corn (*Zea mays*)
- **Disease**: Gray Leaf Spot (GLS)
- **Causal Pathogens**: *Cercospora zeae-maydis* and *Cercospora zeina*
- **Pathogen Classification**: Ascomycete / Mycosphaerellaceae
- **Significance**: One of the leading yield-reducing foliar diseases of corn in minimum-tillage and continuous-corn farming systems.

---

## 2. Key Symptoms & Field Identification
- **Rectangular Lesions (Diagnostic)**: Lesions begin as small, tan to reddish-brown spots with yellow halos. As they elongate, they are strictly delimited by parallel leaf veins, forming distinct **blocky, rectangular, ribbon-like lesions** (1.5 to 6 cm long by 2 to 4 mm wide).
- **Gray Appearance**: During humid conditions, abundant conidiophores emerge through stomata, giving the lesion centers a distinct silvery-gray cast.
- **Canopy Blighting**: Lesions coalesce, killing large swaths of leaf tissue. Premature loss of photosynthetic leaf area leads to stalk cannibalization and lodging.

---

## 3. Favorable Environmental Conditions & Infection Dynamics
- **Temperature Optimum**: Warm conditions: 25°C to 30°C (77°F to 86°F).
- **Moisture Requirements**: High relative humidity > 90% and leaf wetness for 11 to 13 hours.
- **Residue Overwintering**: The fungus overwinters exclusively in corn surface residue (stover), making no-till, continuous corn fields hotbeds for early-season GLS epidemics.
