# Comprehensive Diagnostic & Management Guide: Apple Scab

## 1. Pathogen Overview & Etiology
- **Crop**: Apple (*Malus domestica*) & Crabapple (*Malus* spp.)
- **Disease**: Apple Scab
- **Causal Pathogen**: *Venturia inaequalis* (anamorph *Spilocaea pomi*)
- **Pathogen Classification**: Ascomycete / Pleosporales
- **Significance**: The most economically destructive fungal disease of apples worldwide, reducing fruit marketability through unsightly blemishes, deformation, and premature fruit and leaf drop.

---

## 2. Key Symptoms & Field Identification

### Foliar Symptoms
- **Velvety Olive-Green Spots**: Early season infections appear as circular, olive-green to dull brownish lesions on both leaf surfaces, especially on the underside of young expanding cluster leaves.
- **Velvety Surface**: Lesions possess an olive-green, velvety, or sooty texture composed of dense masses of conidia and conidiophores.
- **Distortion & Leaf Drop**: As lesions age, margins become distinct and darken to metallic olive-black. Severe infection causes leaf curling, yellowing, blistering, and extensive premature mid-summer defoliation.

### Fruit Symptoms
- Diagnostic lesions begin as olive-brown spots that develop a corky, scabby, rough dark brown texture.
- As healthy fruit tissue expands around the rigid, dead infected skin, fruit becomes deformed, misshapen, cracked, and predisposed to secondary storage rots.

## 3. Mills Infection Periods & Weather Dynamics
Apple scab ascospore infection is governed strictly by the **Mills Period** — the duration of continuous leaf wetness required at specific ambient temperatures:

| Average Temperature (°C / °F) | Minimum Hours of Continuous Leaf Wetness for Light Infection |
| :--- | :--- |
| **6°C (43°F)** | 21 hours |
| **10°C (50°F)** | 14 hours |
| **15°C (59°F)** | 9.5 hours |
| **18°C to 24°C (65°F to 75°F)** | **6.0 hours (Optimal Infection Peak)** |
| **26°C (79°F)** | 11 hours |

## 5. Organic IPM & Orchard Sanitation

- **Dormant Urea Treatment**: Apply 5% agricultural urea solution (45 kg/ha) to orchard floor leaves in autumn just before leaf drop to accelerate leaf breakdown and reduce overwintering pseudothecia by up to 90%.
- **Flail Mowing**: Shred fallen leaves with a flail mower in late autumn or early spring to speed microbial degradation of fungal ascocarps.
- **OMRI Sprays**: Apply Liquid Lime Sulfur or Potassium Bicarbonate (3.5 g/L) + Sulfur 80% WDG (3 g/L) during early vegetative development.
- **Resistant Cultivars**: Plant genetically resistant varieties carrying the *Vf* gene (e.g., 'Liberty', 'Freedom', 'Enterprise', 'GoldRush', 'Pristine', 'CrimsonCrisp').
