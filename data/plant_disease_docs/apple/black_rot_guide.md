# Comprehensive Diagnostic & Management Guide: Apple Black Rot & Frogeye Leaf Spot

## 1. Pathogen Overview & Etiology
- **Crop**: Apple (*Malus domestica*)
- **Disease**: Black Rot (Fruit/Canker) & Frogeye Leaf Spot (Foliage)
- **Causal Pathogen**: *Botryosphaeria obtusa* (anamorph *Diplodia seriata*)
- **Pathogen Classification**: Ascomycete / Botryosphaeriaceae
- **Disease Complexes**: Causes three distinct phases: (1) Frogeye Leaf Spot on leaves, (2) Black Rot on fruit, and (3) Perennial limb cankers.

---

## 2. Key Symptoms & Field Identification
- **Frogeye Leaf Spot**: Circular purple specks appear on leaves 1 to 3 weeks after petal fall. Spots expand to 4 to 5 mm with an ashen-grey to tan center encircled by a distinct purple border, creating a characteristic "frogeye" pattern.
- **Fruit Black Rot**: Typically initiates near the calyx end or at insect wounds. Lesions expand as firm, brown rotting zones with alternating light and dark brown concentric bands. Eventually, the entire apple rots, turns coal-black, shrivels into a hard wrinkled "mummy", and clings to the tree branch.
- **Limb Cankers**: Slightly sunken, reddish-brown bark lesions on twigs, branches, and trunk. Cankers expand over seasons, girdling limbs and causing branch dieback.

---

## 3. Integrated Management & Chemical Controls
- **Winter Orchard Sanitation (Critical)**: Prune out and burn all dead wood, fire blight strikes, broken branches, and clinging mummified fruit, which are the primary overwintering sources of pycnidia.
- **Chemical Protectant Rotation**:
  - **Captan 50WP** (2.5 g/L): Excellent protective coverage from pink bud through cover sprays.
  - **Thiophanate-Methyl 70WP** (Topsin-M, 1.5 g/L, FRAC 1): Highly effective for canker prevention and fruit rot control.
  - **Pristine WG** (Pyraclostrobin + Boscalid, 1.0 g/L, FRAC 7 + 11): Applied at petal fall and summer cover sprays.
- **Fire Blight & Insect Vector Control**: Controlling codling moth, plum curculio, and fire blight wounds eliminates entry points for *Botryosphaeria*.
