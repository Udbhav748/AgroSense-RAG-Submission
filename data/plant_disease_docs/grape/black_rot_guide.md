# Comprehensive Diagnostic & Management Guide: Grape Black Rot

## 1. Pathogen Overview & Etiology
- **Crop**: Grape (*Vitis vinifera*, *Vitis labrusca*, and interspecific French-American hybrids)
- **Disease**: Black Rot
- **Causal Pathogen**: *Guignardia bidwellii* (Ellis) Viala & Ravaz (anamorph *Phyllosticta ampelicida*)
- **Pathogen Classification**: Ascomycete / Botryosphaeriales
- **Significance**: Highly destructive disease in warm, humid grape regions, capable of causing 100% crop loss in susceptible cultivars if unchecked.

---

## 2. Key Symptoms & Field Identification
- **Foliar Lesions**: Small, circular, reddish-brown spots appear on young leaves in spring. Spots expand to 3 to 8 mm, turning necrotic tan with a distinct dark brown border. Tiny black pimple-like pycnidia appear in a ring pattern near the outer edge of each spot.
- **Shoot & Cane Cankers**: Elongated, sunken, purple-to-black cankers on young shoots, tendrils, and cluster stems (rachis). Cankers weaken cluster attachments, causing whole bunches to break off.
- **Fruit Infection & Mummy Formation (Diagnostic)**: Berries become susceptible from bloom until pea-size (approximately 4 to 5 weeks post-bloom). Light brown circular soft spots appear on green berries, rapidly engulfing the entire berry within 48 hours. The rotting berry shrivels, turns bluish-black, and becomes a hard, wrinkled **mummy** studded with pycnidia.

## 4. Viticultural Management & Sanitation
- **Winter Mummy Pruning**: Hand-remove and burn or bury all mummies clinging to trellises and on the vineyard floor during winter pruning.
- **Canopy Management**: Shoot positioning, shoot thinning, and selective leaf pulling in the fruiting zone to promote rapid cluster drying.
