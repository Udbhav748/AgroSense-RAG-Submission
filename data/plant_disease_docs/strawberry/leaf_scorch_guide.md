# Comprehensive Diagnostic & Management Guide: Strawberry Leaf Scorch

## 1. Pathogen Overview & Etiology
- **Crop**: Cultivated Strawberry (*Fragaria* x *ananassa*)
- **Disease**: Leaf Scorch
- **Causal Pathogen**: *Diplocarpon earlianum* (Ellis & Everh.) F.A. Wolf (anamorph *Marssonina fragariae*)
- **Classification**: Ascomycete / Dermateaceae
- **Impact**: Attacks leaves, petioles, runners, and fruit calyxes (caps), reducing photosynthetic capacity and causing dead brown "burnt" leaf canopies and dried fruit caps.

---

## 2. Key Symptoms & Field Identification
- **Irregular Purple Blotches (Diagnostic)**: Numerous small, irregular, purple to dark brownish-red spots (1 to 5 mm) develop on upper leaf surfaces. Unlike Strawberry Common Leaf Spot (*Mycosphaerella fragariae*), which develops a white center, Leaf Scorch spots **remain dark purple or dark brown throughout the entire lesion without a white center**.
- **Scorched Foliage Appearance**: Spots enlarge and coalesce until the entire leaf tissue between spots turns reddish-purple, then brown, dry, and brittle. Leaf margins curl upward, giving the entire strawberry bed a charred or "scorched" appearance.
- **Calyx Infection**: The green sepals (cap) surrounding the berry develop dark purple spots and dry up into brown, brittle tissue, significantly reducing fresh market berry grade.

---

## 3. Integrated Disease Management & Fungicide Protocol

| Control Tactic | Specification / Product | Application Details |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Protectant Fungicide** | Captan 50% WP (2.0 to 2.5 g/L) | Multi-site contact protectant applied from early spring emergence through harvest. 0-day PHI. |
| **Systemic Fungicide** | Thiophanate-Methyl 70% WP (Topsin-M, 1.0 to 1.5 g/L) | Apply at first sign of purpling on foliage (FRAC 1). |
| **QoI + DMI Combination** | Pyraclostrobin + Boscalid (Pristine WG, 1.0 g/L) | Exceptional dual-action control of scorch and gray mold (*Botrytis*). |
| **Organic OMRI Fungicide** | Copper Octanoate (Camelot O, 3.0 ml/L) | Liquid copper soap spray for organic strawberry production. |
| **Bed Renovation & Sanitation** | Post-Harvest Mowing | In perennial matted-row plantings, mow old foliage immediately after harvest and rake out debris to lower inoculum. |
