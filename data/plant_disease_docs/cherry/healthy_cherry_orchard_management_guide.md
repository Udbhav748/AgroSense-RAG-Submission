# Comprehensive Agronomic & Orchard Management Guide: Healthy Cherry (*Prunus avium* / *Prunus cerasus*)

## 1. Visual Status & Foliage Assessment
Healthy cherry foliage exhibits deep green, symmetrical, lanceolate leaves with serrated margins and no chlorotic mottling, necrosis, shot-holing, or fungal pustules. The petiole glands are intact and terminal shoot growth is vigorous (typically 12–18 inches of annual new wood on young trees, 8–12 inches on mature trees).

## 2. Preventive Field Protocol & Maintenance Schedule
- **Canopy Aeration & Light Penetration**: Maintain an open-center or central-leader training system. Prune annually during dormant winter or dry summer to enhance airflow through the interior canopy and reduce microclimatic humidity that favors fungal sporulation.
- **Irrigation Management**: Use micro-sprinklers or low-angle drip emitters beneath the drip line. Avoid overhead sprinkler irrigation that wets foliar surfaces during warm evening hours.
- **Nutrient Budgeting**: Apply balanced nitrogen fertilizer based on annual leaf tissue analysis (target leaf N is 2.2–2.6%). Avoid late-summer excessive nitrogen applications that induce succulent, frost-sensitive late flushes prone to fungal infection.

## 3. Organic & Biological Preventive Controls
- **Dormant Horticultural Oil**: Apply superior spray horticultural oil (1.5–2% volume in water) during delayed-dormant stage to smother overwintering scale insects, mites, and aphid eggs.
- **Biological Inoculants**: Apply *Bacillus subtilis* (Serenade ASO / Optiva) or *Trichoderma harzianum* as preventative bio-fungicides during high-risk spring wet periods to establish beneficial phyllosphere colonization.
- **Compost Mulching**: Spread 2–3 inches of well-cured compost or wood chip mulch beneath the tree drip line, keeping mulch 4–6 inches away from the trunk root flare to prevent crown rot (*Phytophthora*).

## 4. Chemical Intervention Protocols
- **Active Treatment**: None needed for visually healthy leaves. Curative synthetic fungicides are NOT warranted on disease-free foliage.
- **Preventive Protection**: During spring bloom (white bud through petal fall), if wet humid weather is forecast, prophylactic sprays of captan (2.0–2.5 lb/acre) or sulfur can protect flowers against brown rot blossom blight (*Monilinia fructicola*).
- **Safety Mandate**: When spraying any dormant oil or protective sulfur, wear standard PPE (chemical-resistant gloves, protective eyewear, long sleeves). Maintain a Minimum Re-Entry Interval (REI) of 12 to 24 hours.

## 5. Orchard Sanitation & Pest Monitoring
- Regularly rake and destroy or chop fallen autumn leaves to eliminate overwintering ascocarps of powdery mildew (*Podosphaera clandestina*) and cherry leaf spot (*Blumeriella jaapii*).
- Hang yellow sticky traps and monitor for Western Cherry Fruit Fly (*Rhagoletis indifferens*) and spotted wing drosophila (SWD) starting around fruit color break.
