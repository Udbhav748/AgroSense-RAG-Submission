# Comprehensive Diagnostic & Management Guide: Potato Late Blight

## 1. Pathogen Overview & Etiology
- **Crop**: Potato (*Solanum tuberosum*)
- **Disease**: Late Blight
- **Causal Pathogen**: *Phytophthora infestans* (Mont.) de Bary
- **Pathogen Classification**: Oomycete / Peronosporales
- **Historical Significance**: The pathogen responsible for the Irish Potato Famine of 1845–1849; remains the number one destructive disease threat in global potato production.

---

## 2. Key Symptoms & Field Identification

### Foliar & Vine Symptoms
- **Water-Soaked Lesions**: Rapidly developing, irregular, dark green to pale green water-soaked spots on leaf tips and margins.
- **Necrotic Lesions**: Expands into large, dark brown to black necrotic areas surrounded by a pale yellowish chlorotic border.
- **White Downy Sporangial Halo**: During periods of high humidity (> 90%) or morning dew, a delicate white mildew-like ring of sporangiophores develops on the underside of leaves at the margin between healthy and dead tissue.
- **Stem Lodging**: Dark brown to purplish-black lesions girdle stems and petioles, making vines brittle and causing complete vine collapse.

### Tuber Symptoms (Tuber Blight)
- **External**: Irregular, slightly sunken areas with brown, reddish, or purplish skin discoloration.
- **Internal**: Dry, granular, reddish-brown to dark brown dry rot extending 5 to 15 mm into the tuber flesh ("rust-colored" rot).
- **Secondary Invasion**: Blight-infected tubers in wet soil are rapidly invaded by *Pectobacterium carotovorum* (bacterial soft rot), transforming tubers into foul-smelling liquid sludge.

---

## 3. Favorable Environmental Conditions & Epidemic Triggers
- **Temperature**: 10°C to 21°C (50°F to 70°F) optimal for sporulation and zoospore release; halted above 30°C.
- **Moisture**: Free water / leaf wetness duration > 8 hours and sustained relative humidity > 90%.
- **Spore Wash-Down**: Rain or sprinkler irrigation washes millions of sporangia/zoospores down from blighted foliage through soil cracks to infect developing tubers.

## 5. Tuber Protection & Harvest Management Protocols
- **Hilling**: Build wide, high soil hills (minimum 10 to 15 cm soil cover over tubers) to physically filter out wash-down spores.
- **Vine Desiccation (Kill-Down)**: Desiccate vines chemically (e.g., Diquat or Glufosinate) or mechanically 2 to 3 weeks before harvest to ensure all foliage is completely dead and dry before digging.
- **Post-Harvest Curing**: Cure harvested potatoes at 10°C to 15°C (50°F to 60°F) with 95% relative humidity and continuous ventilation for 14 days to promote wound suberization and healing before dropping to long-term storage temperatures (3°C to 5°C).
